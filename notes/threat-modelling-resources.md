# Threat-modelling research corpus

> Every source the threat-modelling research lanes actually used, mined from the research
> session transcripts (2026-08). Annotations reflect what each source contributed to the
> analysis. Syntheses of all this live in the two published artifacts — *The Inference
> Dossier* (pedagogy) and *Backdoor & Collusion Trees* (the worked decompositions) — and in
> `module-1-threat-trees.md` / `backdoor-game-flow.md` here. A handful of unglamorous
> reference links (NASA/NRC/FAA handbook PDFs) are labelled generically where the exact
> document title wasn't re-verified.

## 1. AI control — the core corpus

- [The case for ensuring that powerful AIs are controlled](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/kcKrE9mzEHrdqtDpE/the-case-for-ensuring-that-powerful-ais-are-controlled) — Greenblatt & Shlegeris. The foundational control argument; source of the black-box/evaluable framing.
- [AI Control: Improving Safety Despite Intentional Subversion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06942) — the APPS backdoor game; ground truth for every number in the trees (71→19→13, 0.338, 52%/32%, 62/97, 15/100).
- [Prioritizing threats for AI control](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/fCazYoZSSMadiT6sf/prioritizing-threats-for-ai-control) — Greenblatt. Concentrated vs diffuse; the module's threat-prioritization reading.
- [AI catastrophes and rogue deployments](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ceBpLHJDdCt3xfEok/ai-catastrophes-and-rogue-deployments) — Shlegeris. "Attack success rate cubed" lives here — the multiplicative-invariants hope.
- [How can we solve diffuse threats like research sabotage with AI control?](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Mf5Hnpi2KcqZdmFDq/how-can-we-solve-diffuse-threats-like-research-sabotage-with) — Hebbar. The action-count × incriminating-ness frontier; why event-shaped decomposition fails on diffuse threats.
- [How to prevent collusion when using untrusted models to monitor each other](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/GCqoks9eZDfpL8L3Q/how-to-prevent-collusion-when-using-untrusted-models-to) — Shlegeris. Ground truth for Tree 2 (M0–M3e, the rarity asymmetry).
- [Catching AIs red-handed](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/i2nmBfCXnadeGmhzW/catching-ais-red-handed) — why a catch is a win condition.
- [Auditing failures vs concentrated failures](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/hirhSqvEAq7pdnyPG/auditing-failures-vs-concentrated-failures) — the two failure axes behind high/low-stakes.
- [Win/continue/lose scenarios and execute/replace/audit protocols](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/jg3PuE3fYL9jq9zHB/) — Shlegeris. The quantitative win/continue/lose frame.
- [Managing catastrophic misuse without robust AIs](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/KENtuXySHJgxsH2Qk/managing-catastrophic-misuse-without-robust-ais) — misuse-side counterpart.
- [Notes on control evaluations for safety cases](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/3s8PtYbo7rCbho4Ev/notes-on-control-evaluations-for-safety-cases) — Redwood. Safety = minimum over red-team attack strategies; the generating-procedure move.
- [Low-stakes alignment](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/TPan9sQFuPP6jgEJo/low-stakes-alignment) — Christiano. The regime where no single action matters.
- [Preventing model exfiltration with upload limits](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/rf66R4YsrCHgWx9RG/preventing-model-exfiltration-with-upload-limits) — a worked necessary-condition (bandwidth) analysis.
- [To be legible, evidence of misalignment probably has to be behavioral](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/4QRvFCzhFbedmNfp4/to-be-legible-evidence-of-misalignment-probably-has-to-be) — what a catch must produce to move decisions.
- [How will we update about scheming?](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/aEguDPoCzt3287CCD/) — multiplicative-evidence (not condition) decomposition.
- [An introduction to AI sandbagging](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/jsmNCj9QKcfdg8fJk/an-introduction-to-ai-sandbagging) and [AI Sandbagging: Language Models can Strategically Underperform on Evaluations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07358) — why leaves are deceptively satisfiable (models can hit target scores).
- [Adaptive Deployment of Untrusted LLMs Reduces Distributed Threats](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17693) · [Ctrl-Z: Controlling AI Agents via Resampling](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07985) *(see also [2504.10374](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10374))* · [Games for AI Control](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07985) — the Stackelberg formalization (blue commits, red best-responds) · [Diffuse AI Control on Fuzzy Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08892).

## 3. Safety cases & assurance argumentation

- Assurance 2.0 / eliminative argumentation: [Defeaters and Eliminative Argumentation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15800) and [Confidence in Assurance 2.0 Cases](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.10665) — Bloomfield & Rushby ("a defeater does not stand alone; there must be a positive case for it to defeat") · [Rushby, Logic and Epistemology in Safety Cases](https://www.csl.sri.com/users/rushby/papers/safecomp13.pdf).
- Multi-legged arguments: [Bloomfield & Littlewood, multi-legged arguments](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/1601/1/INUCE_multi-leg_v15.2.pdf) · [Confidence: its role in dependability cases](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/1618/1/Confidence_DSN2007v17b_fina.pdf) · [diversity paper (TSE)](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/1619/1/TSE-0219-0805.R1_complete_d.pdf) — why a supportive second leg can reduce confidence; inter-leg correlation.
- [Leveson, The Use of Safety Cases in Certification and Regulation](http://sunnyday.mit.edu/SafetyCases.pdf) — "focus not on showing the system is safe but in attempting to show that it is unsafe."
- [A Causal Framework for AI Regulation and Auditing](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/science/a-causal-framework-for-ai-regulation-and-auditing) — Apollo.
- [Risk assessment at AGI companies](https://www.governance.ai/research-paper/risk-assessment-at-agi-companies-a-review-of-popular-risk-assessment-techniques-from-other-safety-critical-industries) — Koessler & Schuett (survey of classical techniques; does *not* recommend FTA/FMEA wholesale).
- Frontier frameworks read for their threat-model content: [Anthropic RSP](https://www.anthropic.com/rsp) (+ [ASL-3 activation report](https://www.anthropic.com/activating-asl3-report), [deployment safeguards](https://www.anthropic.com/asl3-deployment-safeguards)) · [OpenAI Preparedness Framework v2](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/18a02b5d-6b67-4cec-ab64-68cdfbddebcd/preparedness-framework-v2.pdf) · DeepMind Frontier Safety Framework ([1.0](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/introducing-the-frontier-safety-framework/fsf-technical-report.pdf), [2.0](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/updating-the-frontier-safety-framework/Frontier%20Safety%20Framework%202.0.pdf), [3.1](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/strengthening-our-frontier-safety-framework/frontier-safety-framework_3-1.pdf)) · [An Approach to Technical AGI Safety and Security](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01849) — DeepMind; source of the "decorrelated failure modes" defense-in-depth criterion · [SaferAI ratings](https://ratings.safer-ai.org/anthropic).
