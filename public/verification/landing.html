    Five modules

    Modules 0–1 are the conceptual on-ramp. Module 2 covers the verification infrastructure itself across hardware, cloud, intelligence and human layers. Module 3 red-teams the whole stack through evasion scenarios. Module 4 asks how to layer imperfect mechanisms into a realistic regime, and ends in the capstone.

      Two vocabularies for the same five modules. The constellation labels them the way the skill graph does — Foundations, Supply chain, Mechanisms, Evasion, Design — and the cards above carry the course’s own module titles. The numbers M0–M4 are the same in both, so a star’s module and a card’s module always match even where the words differ.

      Every module ends in writing

      Most modules ask for one short written output — a memo, brief, critique or design note — applied to a concrete verification problem, and put through peer review. The memo desk collects every place the outline marks, quotes the brief where one exists, and says plainly where none has been drafted yet. The capstone bank holds the longer projects the track ends in.
