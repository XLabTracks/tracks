## Session goal

By the end of the meeting, each participant has picked one area of control work
from
[the overview reading](/tracks/control/areas-of-control-work/an-overview-of-areas-of-control-work),
can state its theory of change in their own words, and can name the crux that
would most change its priority. This is a short, discussion-only session — the
module is a single reading.

## Before the session

- Read the overview yourself and note which areas the room is most likely to
  pick, so you can push the discussion toward neglected ones.
- If you run a classroom, check the dashboard for who finished the reading; ask
  two or three finishers beforehand to come with a chosen area.
- Have the [crux exercise](/exercises/c-areas-l1-crux) and the
  [theory-of-change exercise](/exercises/c-areas-l1-theory-of-change) open to
  structure the middle blocks.

## Agenda

Times are a running clock for a 45-minute session. This session is
discussion-only; if it runs short, end early rather than padding.

### Warm-up: the map (0:00–0:10)

- **0:00–0:02** — settle in; state the session goal.
- **0:02–0:07** — list on the whiteboard every area of control work the room
  can recall from the reading, unaided.
- **0:07–0:10** — open the reading's contents on the shared screen and fill
  the gaps together; mark which areas nobody remembered.

Prompts:

- "Which area did nobody remember — and is that about the area or about the
  reading?"
- "Which two areas on this board are closest to being the same thing?"

### Theory-of-change rounds (0:10–0:25)

- **0:10–0:12** — each participant picks one area; no two participants may
  pick the same one until every area has an owner.
- **0:12–0:25** — ninety seconds per speaker, in the shape of
  [the theory-of-change exercise](/exercises/c-areas-l1-theory-of-change):
  what does this work produce, who uses it, and what failure does it prevent?
  One clarifying question from the room per speaker, then next.

Prompts:

- "Who, by name or role, picks up this work's output — a lab team, a
  regulator, another researcher?"
- "What failure happens in the world where nobody does this work?"
- If a speaker describes activity instead of impact: "That's what the work
  is — what does it change?"

### Crux hunt (0:25–0:40)

- **0:25–0:28** — vote by hand count for the three areas the room found most
  compelling.
- **0:28–0:38** — for each of the three, the group names the crux — as in
  [the crux exercise](/exercises/c-areas-l1-crux) — that would most raise or
  lower that area's priority if it resolved. Write each crux next to its area.
- **0:38–0:40** — ask which crux could actually be resolved within a year, and
  by whom.

Prompts:

- "If this crux resolved against the area, would anyone here still work on
  it?"
- "Is this crux empirical or strategic — could an experiment settle it?"
- If cruxes sound vague: "State it as a bet: what result, by when, at what
  odds?"

### Wrap-up (0:40–0:45)

Ask each participant: "If you had three months to contribute to one area on
this board, which one, and what is the first thing you would build?" One
sentence each; collect the answers — they are good seeds for project matching
later in the track.

## Common sticking points

- Participants restate an area's activity instead of its theory of change.
  Push for the causal chain: output → user → prevented failure.
- The room gravitates to the areas that sound most like research they already
  know. Ask explicitly for the least-discussed area on the board.

## After the session

- Post the whiteboard photo (areas + cruxes) to your group's channel.
- On the classroom dashboard, check that participants moved on to the next
  module during the week; this short module is where cohorts most often stall.
