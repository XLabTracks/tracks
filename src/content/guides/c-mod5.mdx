## Session goal

By the end of the meeting, each participant can say what makes a threat
high-stakes, walk the rogue-deployment threat model on a whiteboard, and
explain what resampling adds to blocking as a response to a caught attack. The
session assumes participants have watched
[the opening talk](/tracks/control/module-4/high-stakes-control-talk) and read
at least the first outline section.

## Before the session

- Skim the three outline lessons
  ([what counts as high stakes](/tracks/control/module-4/what-is-high-stakes),
  [monitoring and mitigation](/tracks/control/module-4/monitoring-and-mitigation),
  [environment construction](/tracks/control/module-4/environment-construction))
  and the readings under each, so you can route questions to sources.
- Open [the defer-to-resample demo](/demos/defer-to-resample) and try each
  readout once; you will drive it on a shared screen.
- If you run a classroom, check the dashboard for who reached the two guided
  walkthroughs
  ([untrusted advice](/tracks/control/module-4/untrusted-advice-guided),
  [legibility protocols](/tracks/control/module-4/legibility-guided)); the
  debrief block works best with at least two finishers per group.

## Agenda

### Warm-up: what is high stakes? (10 min)

Ask the room for failures from earlier modules and sort them live into
high-stakes and low-stakes columns on the whiteboard, using the definition from
[the win-continue-lose reading](/tracks/control/module-4/win-continue-lose).
Disputed placements are the interesting ones — let the room argue them.

### Rogue deployments on the whiteboard (15 min)

Have one participant draw the threat model from
[the rogue deployments reading](/tracks/control/module-4/rogue-deployments)
from memory while the room corrects. Then add where
[internal deployments via external APIs](/tracks/control/module-4/rogue-internal-deployments-via-external-apis)
fit on the drawing.

Prompt: "Which arrow on this drawing would you monitor first, and what would
that monitor actually see?"

### Resampling, live (15 min)

Step through [the defer-to-resample demo](/demos/defer-to-resample) on the
shared screen. Before each readout, ask the room to predict whether the
protocol wins, continues, or loses, and why. Connect the mechanics back to
[the Ctrl-Z reading](/tracks/control/module-4/ctrl-z-resampling).

### Walkthrough debrief (15 min)

In two groups, one per guided walkthrough
([untrusted advice](/tracks/control/module-4/untrusted-advice-guided),
[legibility protocols](/tracks/control/module-4/legibility-guided)): each group
reconstructs the decision it was forced to make and reports whether the
walkthrough's evidence changed its members' initial choice. Compare the two —
one is argued, one is measured.

### Wrap-up (5 min)

Each participant names the mitigation from this module they'd implement first
in a real lab, in one sentence. Collect open questions.

## Common sticking points

- "High stakes" gets read as "high importance". Send the room back to the
  definition in the first outline section: it is about what one uncaught
  action can do.
- The rogue-deployment drawing sprawls. Keep the whiteboard version to the
  boxes and arrows the reading itself names.
- The demo invites button-mashing. Force a prediction before every readout;
  the prediction, not the readout, is the exercise.

## After the session

- On the classroom dashboard, check who completed the two guided walkthroughs
  after the meeting; the debrief usually motivates the holdouts.
- The module's readings carry spaced-repetition recall cards; remind
  participants that the review tab resurfaces what this session touched.
