## Session goal

By the end of the meeting, each participant can say what makes a threat
high-stakes, walk the rogue-deployment threat model on a whiteboard, and
explain what resampling adds to blocking as a response to a caught attack. The
session assumes participants have watched
[the opening talk](/tracks/control/module-4/high-stakes-control-talk) and read
at least the first outline section.

## Before the session

- Skim the three outline lessons
  ([what counts as high stakes](/tracks/control/module-4/what-is-high-stakes),
  [monitoring and mitigation](/tracks/control/module-4/monitoring-and-mitigation),
  [environment construction](/tracks/control/module-4/environment-construction))
  and the readings under each, so you can route questions to sources.
- Open [the defer-to-resample demo](/demos/defer-to-resample) and try each
  readout once; you will drive it on a shared screen.
- If you run a classroom, check the dashboard for who reached the two guided
  walkthroughs
  ([collusion prevention](/tracks/control/module-4/collusion-guided),
  [legibility protocols](/tracks/control/module-4/legibility-guided)); the
  debrief block works best with at least two finishers per group.

## Agenda

Times are a running clock for a 60-minute session; if a block runs over, take
the time from the resampling block — the walkthrough debrief needs its full
fifteen minutes.

### Warm-up: what is high stakes? (0:00–0:10)

Draw two empty columns (high-stakes / low-stakes) on the whiteboard. The room
supplies failures from earlier modules; sort them live into the columns using
the definition from
[the win-continue-lose reading](/tracks/control/module-4/win-continue-lose).
Argue the disputed placements — those are the interesting ones.

Prompts:

- "What one feature of this failure decides its column?"
- "Can the same failure sit in different columns at different labs? What
  changed?"
- If sorting is too easy: "Construct a failure that genuinely straddles the
  line."

### Rogue deployments on the whiteboard (0:10–0:25)

One participant draws the threat model from
[the rogue deployments reading](/tracks/control/module-4/rogue-deployments)
from memory; the room corrects out loud as they go. Then add where
[internal deployments via external APIs](/tracks/control/module-4/rogue-internal-deployments-via-external-apis)
fit on the drawing, and finish with the monitoring discussion (prompts below).

Prompts:

- "Which arrow on this drawing would you monitor first, and what would that
  monitor actually see?"
- "Which box on this drawing does the
  [systems-architecture reading](/tracks/control/module-4/systems-architecture)
  live in?"
- If the drawing stalls: hand the marker to a second participant and let the
  first narrate instead.

### Resampling, live (0:25–0:40)

Recap blocking as the baseline response, from
[the monitoring section](/tracks/control/module-4/monitoring-and-mitigation).
Then step through [the defer-to-resample demo](/demos/defer-to-resample) on
the shared screen — before every readout, the room predicts win, continue, or
lose, and says why, before you click. Connect the mechanics back to
[the Ctrl-Z reading](/tracks/control/module-4/ctrl-z-resampling).

Prompts:

- "What does resampling buy that blocking doesn't? What does it cost?"
- "When would you rather block than resample, and who makes that call in
  practice?"
- If predictions are all correct: "Which parameter of this demo would you
  change to make your predictions fail?"

### Walkthrough debrief (0:40–0:55)

Split into two groups, one per guided walkthrough:
[collusion prevention](/tracks/control/module-4/collusion-guided) and
[legibility protocols](/tracks/control/module-4/legibility-guided). Each group
reconstructs the decision its walkthrough forced and whether the walkthrough's
evidence changed its members' initial choice. Groups then report to each
other; compare the two head-on — one is argued, one is measured.

Prompts:

- "What was the exact moment in the walkthrough where your choice flipped, if
  it did?"
- "Which would you trust more in a real deployment decision: the argument or
  the measurement? Why?"
- "What additional experiment would have settled your walkthrough's question?"

### Wrap-up (0:55–1:00)

Each participant names the mitigation from this module they would implement
first in a real lab — one sentence, no discussion. Collect open questions.

Prompt: "What is the cheapest thing this module described that a lab could
ship this quarter?"

## After the session

- On the classroom dashboard, check who completed the two guided walkthroughs
  after the meeting; the debrief usually motivates the holdouts.
- The module's readings carry spaced-repetition recall cards; remind
  participants that the review tab resurfaces what this session touched.
