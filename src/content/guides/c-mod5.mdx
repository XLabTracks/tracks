## Before the session

- Skim the three outline lessons ([what counts as high stakes](/tracks/control/module-4/what-is-high-stakes), [monitoring and mitigation](/tracks/control/module-4/monitoring-and-mitigation), [environment construction](/tracks/control/module-4/environment-construction)) and the readings under each, so you can route questions to sources.
- Open [the defer-to-resample demo](/demos/defer-to-resample) and try each readout once; you will drive it on a shared screen.
- If you run a classroom, check the dashboard for who reached the two guided walkthroughs ([collusion prevention](/tracks/control/module-4/collusion-guided), [legibility protocols](/tracks/control/module-4/legibility-guided))

## Agenda

Times are a running clock for a 60-minute session
### Warm-up: what is high stakes? (0:00–0:10)

Draw two empty columns (high-stakes / low-stakes) on the whiteboard. The room supplies failures from earlier modules; sort them live into the columns using the definition from [the win-continue-lose reading](/tracks/control/module-4/win-continue-lose). 

Prompts:
- "Which column does this failure go in?"
- "Are there failures that are ambiguous, what makes it this way?"

### Rogue deployments on the whiteboard (0:10–0:25)

One participant draws the threat model from [the rogue deployments reading](/tracks/control/module-4/rogue-deployments) from memory; the room corrects out loud as they go. Then add where [internal deployments via external APIs](/tracks/control/module-4/rogue-internal-deployments-via-external-apis) fit on the drawing, and finish with the monitoring discussion (prompts below).

Prompts:

- "Which arrow on this drawing would you monitor first, and what would that monitor actually see?"
- If the drawing stalls: hand the marker to a second participant and let the first narrate instead.

### Resampling, live (0:25–0:40)

Recap blocking as the baseline response, from [the monitoring section](/tracks/control/module-4/monitoring-and-mitigation). Then step through [the defer-to-resample demo](/demos/defer-to-resample) on the shared screen — before every readout, the room predicts win, continue, or lose, and says why, before you click. Connect the mechanics back to [the Ctrl-Z reading](/tracks/control/module-4/ctrl-z-resampling).

Prompt:
- "When would you rather block than resample?"

### Walkthrough debrief (0:40–0:55)

Split into two groups, one per guided walkthrough: [collusion prevention](/tracks/control/module-4/collusion-guided) and [legibility protocols](/tracks/control/module-4/legibility-guided). Each group reconstructs the decision its walkthrough forced and whether the walkthrough's evidence changed its members' initial choice. Groups then report to each other; compare the two head-on.

Prompts:
- "What was the exact moment in the walkthrough where your choice flipped, if it did?"
- "What additional experiment would have settled your walkthrough's question?"

### Wrap-up (0:55–1:00)

Each participant names the mitigation from this module they would implement first in a real lab. Collect open questions.

Prompt: "What is the cheapest thing this module described that a lab could ship this quarter?"

## After the session

- On the classroom dashboard, check who completed the two guided walkthroughs after the meeting; the debrief usually motivates the holdouts.
- The module's readings carry spaced-repetition recall cards; remind participants that the review tab resurfaces what this session touched.
