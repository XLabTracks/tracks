## Before the session

- Click through [the seeker carousel demo](/demos/seeker-carousel) once; you
  will use it as the warm-up anchor.
- Reread [the deals reading](/tracks/control/module-5/making-deals-with-early-schemers)
  far enough to moderate the negotiation block, and skim
  [the barriers reading](/tracks/control/module-5/barriers-to-trading).
- If you run a classroom, check the dashboard for who reached
  [the trading-with-AIs section](/tracks/control/module-5/trading-with-ais);
  the negotiation block needs participants who have seen the deal structure.

## Agenda

Times are a running clock for a 60-minute session. Decide before the session
whether the middle block is the roleplay or the memo variant — don't decide
in the room.

### Warm-up: who else is in the zoo? (0:00–0:10)

Step through [the seeker carousel demo](/demos/seeker-carousel) on the shared
screen. For each archetype, the room answers the first prompt below before you
advance.

Prompts:

- "What would this archetype do in the control game from module 1 that a
  schemer would not?"
- "What evidence would distinguish this archetype from a schemer in
  deployment?"
- If answers blur together: "Which of module 1's countermeasures does nothing
  against this one?"

### Satiation and spillways (0:10–0:20)

In pairs: one participant explains
[the satiation reading](/tracks/control/module-5/satiating-ai-preferences),
the other
[the spillway reading](/tracks/control/module-5/spillway-motivation) — two
minutes each, partner summarizes it back in one sentence. Pairs then compare:
what problem does each solve, and for which archetype? Use
[the spillway-routing demo](/demos/spillway-routing) on the shared screen to
settle mechanism disagreements.

Prompts:

- "Which archetype from the warm-up does each of these address — and which
  archetype does neither touch?"
- "What would it look like for this mechanism to fail quietly?"

### The negotiation (0:20–0:45)

Roleplay variant: three groups — the lab, the early schemer's interests, the
credibility observers. Recap the deal structure from
[the deals reading](/tracks/control/module-5/making-deals-with-early-schemers),
then the lab drafts a concrete offer (what is paid, when, verified how) while
the AI side separately lists what it would actually take. Negotiate across the
table with the observers noting which promises either side should believe;
finish with the observers reporting and the room scoring the final offer's
credibility in both directions.

Memo variant (choose beforehand): groups run
[the offer-memo exercise](/exercises/c-paper-deals-write-offer-memo), then
compare memos line by line in the last few minutes of the block.

Prompts:

- "What is the payment, exactly — and what stops the lab from withholding it
  after the AI delivers?"
- "How does either side verify the other performed? Name the mechanism."
- "What does the AI side actually want that the lab can grant?"
- If the negotiation stalls: "Lower the stakes — what is the smallest deal
  both sides would sign today?"

### Barriers (0:45–0:55)

List on the whiteboard the barriers the room hit during the negotiation, in
the room's own words. Then check the list against
[the barriers reading](/tracks/control/module-5/barriers-to-trading); mark
which barrier the roleplay reproduced most faithfully and which the room never
hit.

Prompts:

- "Which barrier on this board is about capability, and which about trust?"
- "Which barrier would remain even with a perfectly honest lab?"

### Wrap-up (0:55–1:00)

Each participant answers in one sentence: "Did this module make deals seem
more workable or less, and what single fact would flip you?" No discussion;
collect open questions.

## After the session

- On the classroom dashboard, check who goes on to the empirics section
  ([reward-seeker empirics](/tracks/control/module-5/reward-seeker-empirics))
  during the week.
- If groups wrote offer memos, read them and pick the two most different to
  open the next session with.
