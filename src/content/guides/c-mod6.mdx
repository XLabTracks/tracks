## Session goal

By the end of the meeting, each participant can name at least two seeker
archetypes and how they differ from schemers, explain what a deal with an AI
is supposed to buy, and state the barrier to trading they find hardest to
overcome. The session assumes participants have read
[the alternatives-to-schemers lesson](/tracks/control/module-5/alternatives-to-schemers).

## Before the session

- Click through [the seeker carousel demo](/demos/seeker-carousel) once; you
  will use it as the warm-up anchor.
- Reread [the deals reading](/tracks/control/module-5/making-deals-with-early-schemers)
  far enough to moderate the negotiation block, and skim
  [the barriers reading](/tracks/control/module-5/barriers-to-trading).
- If you run a classroom, check the dashboard for who reached
  [the trading-with-AIs section](/tracks/control/module-5/trading-with-ais);
  the negotiation block needs participants who have seen the deal structure.

## Agenda

### Warm-up: who else is in the zoo? (10 min)

Step through [the seeker carousel demo](/demos/seeker-carousel) on the shared
screen. For each archetype, ask the room: "What would this one do in the
control game from module 1 that a schemer would not?"

### Satiation and spillways (10 min)

In pairs: one participant explains the idea in
[the satiation reading](/tracks/control/module-5/satiating-ai-preferences),
the other the idea in
[the spillway reading](/tracks/control/module-5/spillway-motivation), each in
two minutes, then they compare: what problem does each solve, and for which
archetype? Use [the spillway-routing demo](/demos/spillway-routing) to settle
disagreements about the mechanism.

### The negotiation (25 min)

Roleplay from [the deals reading](/tracks/control/module-5/making-deals-with-early-schemers):
one group plays the lab, one plays the early schemer's interests, one observes
credibility. The lab drafts a concrete offer (what is paid, when, verified
how), the AI side says what it would actually take, the observers judge which
promises either side should believe. If the group prefers structure over
roleplay, run [the offer-memo exercise](/exercises/c-paper-deals-write-offer-memo)
in groups instead and compare memos.

### Barriers (10 min)

List on the whiteboard the barriers the room hit during the negotiation, then
check them against [the barriers reading](/tracks/control/module-5/barriers-to-trading)
and [the cooperation reading](/tracks/control/module-5/cooperating-with-unaligned-ais).
Which barrier did the roleplay reproduce most faithfully?

### Wrap-up (5 min)

Each participant answers in one sentence: "Did this module make deals seem
more workable or less, and what single fact would flip you?"

## Common sticking points

- Everything gets re-explained as scheming. The module's point is the space of
  non-schemer motivations; when a participant collapses an archetype into a
  schemer, ask what evidence would distinguish the two.
- The negotiation drifts into science fiction. Keep the offer concrete: an
  amount, a trigger, a verification step — the reading's own frame.
- Credibility runs one-way. Ask both directions explicitly: why should the AI
  believe the lab, and why should the lab believe the AI?

## After the session

- On the classroom dashboard, check who goes on to the empirics section
  ([reward-seeker empirics](/tracks/control/module-5/reward-seeker-empirics))
  during the week.
- If groups wrote offer memos, read them and pick the two most different to
  open the next session with.
