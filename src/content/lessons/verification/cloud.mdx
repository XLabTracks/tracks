## Module objective

The provider sits between the customer and the machines. This unit asks what the landlord of the compute can see, record, verify and switch off — and where that oversight runs out.

## Sources

Read these four. Everything below is answerable from them.

<ReadingCard id="cloud-heim-governing" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08501" title="Governing Through the Cloud: The Intermediary Role of Compute Providers in AI Regulation" author="Heim, Fist, Egan et al." year="2024" mins="the backbone">

The four capacities a provider can serve a governance regime in — **securer, record keeper, verifier, enforcer** — and the case that non-confidential metadata already shows workload scale, and often type, without anyone reading a customer's code or data. Page 31 carries the EO 14110 reporting thresholds two of the questions turn on.

</ReadingCard>

<ReadingCard id="cloud-egan-heim-kyc" href="https://cdn.governance.ai/Oversight_for_Frontier_AI_through_a_KYC_Scheme_for_Compute_Providers.pdf" title="Oversight for Frontier AI through a Know-Your-Customer Scheme for Compute Providers" author="Egan and Heim" year="2023" mins="the machinery">

What it takes to know who the customer actually is: verified identity, beneficial ownership through layered holding companies, declared purpose, reporting duties, suspension — and the paper's own condition that coverage has to spread across jurisdictions or the checkpoint becomes a detour sign.

</ReadingCard>

<ReadingCard id="cloud-rand-detection-gaps" href="https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA3686-1.html" title="Strategies and Detection Gaps in a Game-Theoretic Model of Compute Governance" author="Moon, Vedula, Geneson and Bar-on, RAND Corporation" year="2025" mins="the counterweight">

The result that a monitor watching only a per-run FLOP threshold provably loses to sequential training, and the band of model sizes where that gap opens.

</ReadingCard>

<ReadingCard id="cloud-carnegie-geopolitics" href="https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/05/the-geopolitical-debates-over-controlling-cloud-compute" title="The Geopolitical Debates Over Controlling Cloud Compute" author="Tan, Carnegie Endowment for International Peace" year="2026" mins="the politics">

Who bears the burden of cloud controls, what over-broad rules cost, and the visibility-against-privacy argument that decides how much of this layer is politically available at all.

</ReadingCard>

<Check
  id="c22-signature"
  q="A workload holds accelerator cores near peak utilization, day and night, for three weeks, with almost no traffic to the outside internet. What does that pattern most suggest?"
  options={[
    "An inference service with many users",
    "A large training run",
    "Nothing — metadata cannot distinguish workload types",
    "Ordinary database hosting",
  ]}
  answer={1}
  why="Sustained flat-out utilization in a closed loop is the training signature; inference load follows users and fluctuates. “Nothing” undersells metadata — estimation is exactly what it supports — and databases do not pin accelerators for weeks."
/>

<Check
  id="c22-thresholds"
  q="Under the EO 14110 template, which threshold is which?"
  options={[
    "Clusters register at 10²⁶ OP; training runs are declared at 10²⁰ OP/s",
    "Both use the same 10²⁶ threshold",
    "Clusters register by capability rate (10²⁰ OP/s); training runs are declared by total compute (10²⁶ OP)",
    "Registration and declaration are two names for the same report",
  ]}
  answer={2}
  why="Standing infrastructure is measured by what it could compute per second, a rate; a run by what it did compute in total. Mixing the two numbers up is the most common error in this unit, and they hook different obligations."
/>

<Check
  id="c22-beneficial-ownership"
  q="A customer entity is owned 100% by a holding company, which is owned by another company offshore. What does beneficial-ownership verification require?"
  options={[
    "Verifying the holding company's name and stopping there",
    "Confirming the customer's email domain matches its website",
    "Checking that the offshore jurisdiction has a tax treaty",
    "Tracing through the layers to the actual humans who own or control the customer",
  ]}
  answer={3}
  why="Beneficial ownership means the natural persons at the end of the chain. Layered holding companies are precisely the structure the requirement exists to see through — stopping at the first shell is the failure mode, not the rule."
/>

<Check
  id="c22-kyc-leak"
  q="Why does a strict KYC scheme in one country alone tend to leak?"
  options={[
    "Because KYC is technically impossible to implement",
    "Customers with something to hide simply move to providers in jurisdictions without the scheme",
    "Because compute cannot be metered per customer",
    "Because the scheme only applies to small customers",
  ]}
  answer={1}
  why="The scheme is sound where it applies — the leak is jurisdictional migration. Per-customer metering is exactly what providers already do for billing, and thresholds target the largest customers, not the smallest."
/>

<Check
  id="c22-sequential"
  q="Why does splitting one training run into 58 sub-threshold chunks defeat a FLOP-threshold monitor?"
  options={[
    "Because splitting reduces the total compute the model needs",
    "Because each chunk becomes technically impossible to meter",
    "The threshold is evaluated per account, no chunk crosses it, and the monitor cannot link the accounts",
    "Because thresholds only apply to inference workloads",
  ]}
  answer={2}
  why="The total compute is unchanged and every chunk is metered perfectly well — the failure is aggregation. Per-account evaluation plus unlinkable accounts means no single reading ever trips the line. Thresholds also target training, not inference."
/>

<Check
  id="c22-outside"
  q="Which of these is fully outside what cloud-layer oversight can see?"
  options={[
    "A declared run at a covered provider",
    "An undeclared run at a covered provider",
    "A customer who reserves compute at a covered provider and never uses it",
    "A training run on a company's own air-gapped cluster",
  ]}
  answer={3}
  why="The covered provider sees declared runs, undeclared runs as telemetry, and idle reservations alike. A self-owned, disconnected cluster has no landlord at all — finding that one belongs to the hardware and intelligence layers, not this one."
/>
