## Sources

<ReadingCard id="cloud-heim-governing" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08501" title="Governing Through the Cloud: The Intermediary Role of Compute Providers in AI Regulation" author="Heim, Fist, Egan et al." year="2024">

The four capacities a provider can serve a governance regime in — **securer, record keeper, verifier, enforcer** — and the case that non-confidential metadata already shows workload scale, and often type, without anyone reading a customer’s code or data.

</ReadingCard>

<ReadingCard id="cloud-egan-heim-kyc" href="https://cdn.governance.ai/Oversight_for_Frontier_AI_through_a_KYC_Scheme_for_Compute_Providers.pdf" title="Oversight for Frontier AI through a Know-Your-Customer Scheme for Compute Providers" author="Egan and Heim" year="2023">

What it takes to know who the customer actually is: verified identity, beneficial ownership through layered holding companies, declared purpose, reporting duties, suspension — and the paper’s own condition that coverage has to spread across jurisdictions.

</ReadingCard>

<ReadingCard id="cloud-rand-detection-gaps" href="https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA3686-1.html" title="Strategies and Detection Gaps in a Game-Theoretic Model of Compute Governance" author="Moon, Vedula, Geneson and Bar-on, RAND Corporation" year="2025">

The result that a monitor watching only a per-run FLOP threshold loses to sequential training, and the band of model sizes where that gap opens.

</ReadingCard>

<ReadingCard id="cloud-carnegie-geopolitics" href="https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/05/the-geopolitical-debates-over-controlling-cloud-compute" title="The Geopolitical Debates Over Controlling Cloud Compute" author="Tan, Carnegie Endowment for International Peace" year="2026">

Who bears the burden of cloud controls, what over-broad rules cost, and the visibility-against-privacy argument that decides how much of this layer is politically available at all.

</ReadingCard>
