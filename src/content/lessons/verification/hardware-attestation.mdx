### 2.1.0 The chip says “compliant”

Runtime: 15 minutes Hard idea: A signed hardware statement and treaty compliance are different propositions.

#### Opening puzzle

Existing U.S.-China emergency-pause design case.

A three-month agreement prohibits unlicensed training above threshold T. Inference and approved safety evaluations remain permitted. One party submits valid attestation tokens from 20,000 accelerators and states that the cluster complied.

Before giving any definitions, learners decide which conclusions the tokens support:

- These are genuine covered devices.
- Their certificates have not been revoked.
- They are running approved firmware or configurations.
- The devices were connected in the declared cluster topology.
- They performed inference rather than training.
- The total training compute remained below T.
- No unregistered accelerators performed another run.
- The treaty authority can suspend their operation.
The learner must select each claim as:

- supported;
- possibly supported, depending on additional design choices;
- unsupported by the artifact.

#### Reveal

Use a simplified, annotated version of an actual NVIDIA attestation token and architecture diagram.

Current NVIDIA documentation describes an attestation chain in which evidence is collected from a GPU, compared with signed reference measurements, checked against certificate-revocation information, and returned as a signed result. Current Blackwell multi-GPU attestation still attests each GPU independently and explicitly does not attest cluster topology or switches. That gives the exercise a real, current boundary: per-device attestation does not automatically establish what the cluster did. (NVIDIA Docs)

#### Advance organizer

After the reveal, introduce the section’s five-function map:

- Identify and account for hardware Which device is this? Is it registered? Where is it supposed to be?
- Attest state and configuration What hardware, firmware, software, or security state is present?
- Measure and classify use How much compute occurred? Was it training, inference, evaluation, or another workload?
- Authorize or restrict use Was the activity permitted? Who can issue, suspend, revoke, or override authorization?
- Reconstruct a declared run Did the declared training process produce the claimed checkpoints, model, or outputs?
Add a visible warning:

These are functions, not a progression from weak to strong. A mechanism may perform one function well and contribute almost nothing to another.

#### Connection to prior modules

The debrief should explicitly retrieve earlier tools:

- Module 0: The treaty goal is a safe pause; the rule concerns above-threshold training; the attestation token tests a narrower claim.
- Module 1: The chip vendor, operator, cloud provider, regulator, and treaty verifier hold different parts of the evidence and authority.
- Module 2.0: The token may preserve workload confidentiality, but confidentiality does not establish completeness.
