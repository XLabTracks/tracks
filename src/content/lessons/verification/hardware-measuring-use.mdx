### 2.1.4 Measuring and classifying use

Runtime: 50 minutes Hard idea: “How much compute occurred?”, “what kind of workload was it?”, and “was it allowed?” are three separate questions.

#### 2.1.4A Compute accounting and workload classification

Runtime: 30 minutes

##### Claim decomposition

Use the working rule:

No party may conduct an unlicensed training run above threshold T during the pause.

A verifier would need at least:

- covered device identity;
- a method for aggregating activity across devices and time;
- a trustworthy measure or bound on compute used;
- a method for distinguishing covered training from permitted activity;
- evidence that cannot be silently deleted, rolled back, replayed, or reassigned;
- a rule for distributed, interrupted, resumed, or sequential runs;
- an actor authorized to decide whether the evidence crosses the legal threshold.

#### Compute measurement

Teach the policy-relevant issues:

- Total operations and throughput are different quantities.
- A counter must define which operations count and how duplicated, sparse, low-precision, or recomputed operations are treated.
- Per-chip records must be aggregated across the relevant run, customer, cluster, sites, and time window.
- A meter can undercount through omission or tampering and can overcount through padding or inefficient computation.
- A precise hardware counter can still operationalize a weak rule if improvements in algorithms or data move dangerous capabilities below the threshold.

The learner does not need microarchitectural detail. The useful question is:

What quantity did the meter record, and how closely does that quantity match the legal rule?

#### Training-versus-inference evidence packet

Give learners a compact packet from the June 2026 telemetry study:

- abstract and headline result;
- one table showing adversarial strategies;
- the section specifying the extra hardware assumptions;
- the limitations section.

The study reports 98.2 percent overall classification accuracy in its corpus and materially lower performance against some difficult, previously unseen adversarial workloads. Its experiments were limited to single-node configurations of up to eight GPUs, and its governance conclusion depends on a tamper-resistant telemetry path, an authenticated channel, and protected monitoring software. Some inference configurations also occupy the same simple arithmetic-intensity range as training, which is why static “training looks busy” heuristics are inadequate. (arXiv)

#### Activity: From paper result to policy claim

Learners complete four statements:

- Observation: What did the experiment measure?
- Supported inference: What result is justified within the experimental setting?
- Unsupported policy leap: Which stronger claim would exceed the evidence?
- Deployment dependencies: What additional hardware and institutional components would be needed?

A strong answer should resemble:

Under the tested hardware, workloads, adversarial strategies, and untampered telemetry assumptions, temporal GPU telemetry carried substantial information about whether training occurred. The study does not establish treaty-grade detection across frontier-scale, multi-node systems or against a state controlling the measurement path.

This directly reuses Module 1’s upstream-source discipline.

#### Adversary’s best response

Give the learner a constrained menu:

- dilute training with benign work;
- manipulate utilization or timing;
- design custom kernels;
- fragment activity across devices or sites;
- forge the telemetry source;
- replay a permitted trace;
- replace the classifier;
- shift to hardware outside the monitored family.

The learner must identify:

- which response attacks the classifier;
- which attacks the evidence channel;
- which attacks coverage;
- which requires another module’s evidence stream.

This prevents the common mistake of treating every weakness as a machine-learning classification problem.

Runtime: 20 minutes

#### Core distinction

A monitor produces evidence. A control mechanism changes what the machine can do. A complete policy still needs an authority that decides when control is justified.

Present the authorization chain:

rule → license criteria → issuer → hardware verification → permitted operation → expiration or renewal → suspension or revocation → appeal or override

Questions learners must ask:

- Who can issue the authorization?
- Does one government, both parties, or a multiparty authority hold the relevant key?
- What happens if the authorization service is unreachable?
- Does the device fail open or fail closed?
- Can an emergency suspension occur before adjudication?
- Who can reverse a mistaken suspension?
- Can a vendor or government disable lawful activity unilaterally?
- What happens after key compromise?
- Can authorization be applied to existing hardware through firmware, or does it require redesigned chips?
- Which party bears the economic cost of false denials or downtime?

Offline licensing and embedded throttling proposals illustrate the distinction between a plausible technical control and an accepted governance arrangement. Petrie’s firmware-based design is explicitly a proposal, with its implementation timeline presented as an author estimate and with physical attacks remaining outside its strongest assurances. It should be analyzed as a candidate deployment pathway, not presented as a currently operating control regime. (arXiv)

#### Interactive: Build the claim chain

Learners receive components and must assemble an end-to-end system for the working treaty rule:

- hardware-rooted identity;
- attested firmware;
- operation counter;
- training classifier;
- signed, nonce-bound record;
- cross-chip aggregation;
- license token;
- revocation list;
- national regulator;
- international notification;
- inspection trigger;
- independent power meter.

After assembling the chain, the system asks:

- Which component measures the prohibited activity?
- Which component merely authenticates another component?
- Which institution determines that the threshold was crossed?
- Which component can stop the activity?
- Which components fail together if the manufacturer’s root key is compromised?
- Which part detects an unregistered cluster?

The intended realization is that several apparently distinct hardware mechanisms can share one common-mode trust failure.

#### Notebook output: Mechanism dossier

The learner completes one compact dossier:

- policy goal;
- legal rule;
- precise hardware-supported claim;
- function: identify, attest, measure, classify, restrict, or reconstruct;
- evidence produced;
- trust chain;
- maturity stage;
- implementation dependencies;
- cooperation required;
- strongest bypass;
- residual blind spot;
- corroborating layer;
- evidence that would change the assessment.
