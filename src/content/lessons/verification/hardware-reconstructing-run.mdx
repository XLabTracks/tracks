### 2.1.7 Optional extension: reconstructing a declared training run

Runtime: 35 to 45 minutes Status: Advanced extension Hard idea: Evidence that a declared run occurred as claimed does not establish that no other run occurred.

#### Scope

Cover:

- Training records;
- Checkpoints or weight snapshots;
- Authenticated transcripts;
- Sampled re-execution;
- Probabilistic recomputation;
- Dedicated verification clusters;
- Proof-of-learning or proof-of-training approaches;
- Confidentiality and spoofing risks.

#### Anchor proposal

Shavit’s compute-monitoring proposal decomposes verification into on-chip weight snapshots, records intended to support later proof of the training process, and supply-chain monitoring intended to prevent a prover from accumulating untracked chips. The paper explicitly presents a high-level system design with open technical problems rather than a deployment-ready mechanism. (ar5iv)

#### Intended discovery

Learners should separate:

- Declared-run correctness: the submitted model or checkpoint plausibly arose from the declared process;
- Declared-run completeness: all relevant activity associated with the declared run has been shown;
- Fleet completeness: no separate prohibited run occurred on other hardware.

Proof-of-learning and recomputation primarily address the first, with varying assurance. They do not automatically establish the third.

#### Exercise: Buy assurance with a verification budget

Give learners a declared training transcript and a limited verification-compute budget.

They choose among:

- Re-running the full job;
- Sampling random segments;
- Checking checkpoints;
- Validating code and data commitments;
- Checking physical compute totals;
- Comparing telemetry and transcript timing;
- Inspecting a random sample of chips.

For each choice, they estimate:

- Cost;
- Confidentiality exposure;
- Spoofing opportunity;
- Detection probability or qualitative assurance;
- Which claim remains untested.

The answer key should explain why a cheap sample can provide useful probabilistic evidence while leaving deliberate transcript construction, precomputed outputs, hidden activity, and unmonitored hardware as distinct problems.

#### Placement of evaluation and inference verification

A brief transfer card can show that attestation, confidential computing, and controlled verification hardware may also help verify model evaluations or inference. Deep treatment should remain outside the 2.1 core:

- The confidentiality and evaluator-access problem fits naturally in 2.0;
- Model-evaluation validity belongs with the evaluation track or linked material;
- Large-scale inference introduces replication, model-substitution, and sensitive-user-data problems that would overload the hardware week.

The core should acknowledge these applications without pretending that training verification covers them.
