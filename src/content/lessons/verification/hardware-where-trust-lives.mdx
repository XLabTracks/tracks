### 2.1.6 Where should trust live?

Runtime: 35 minutes Hard idea: On-chip and off-chip mechanisms offer different trust distributions. Combining them helps only when their failure modes are meaningfully independent.

#### Advance comparison

Present the same verification target:

Determine whether all covered compute in a declared data center is accounted for by permitted workloads.

Then compare three architectures.

#### Architecture A: On-chip monitoring

Possible components:

- device identity;
- secure or measured boot;
- signed counters;
- protected telemetry;
- workload certificates;
- hardware-backed authorization.

Potential advantages:

- fine-grained access to device state and activity;
- low marginal cost after deployment across a fleet;
- evidence travels with the chip;
- possible real-time response;
- privacy-preserving outputs.

Load-bearing concerns:

- trust in chip design and manufacturing;
- vendor control of keys, reference values, firmware, and updates;
- physical access by the prover;
- legacy and nonparticipating hardware;
- redesign and fleet-turnover timelines;
- common-mode failure across identity, metering, and licensing.

#### Architecture B: Off-chip digital monitoring

Possible components:

- network taps;
- input/output loggers;
- server or rack gateways;
- tamper-evident enclosures;
- separate secured verification machines.

Potential advantages:

- devices can be jointly designed and mutually inspected;
- reduced reliance on the monitored party’s accelerator internals;
- possible retrofitting of existing systems;
- clearer separation between prover hardware and verifier hardware.

Load-bearing concerns:

- reduced visibility as the tap moves farther from the chip;
- encrypted traffic;
- data volumes;
- covert and side channels;
- installation and chain-of-custody requirements;
- risk that the verifier’s device contains espionage or sabotage functions;
- difficulty reconstructing high-level workloads from low-level traffic.

The six-layer synthesis explicitly treats network taps and analog sensors as separate off-chip approaches and proposes mutually vetted, simple, potentially jointly developed devices. It also emphasizes that these approaches still face major unsolved engineering and assurance problems. (arXiv)

A June 2026 proposal makes the concept more concrete by placing taps on information-carrying connections, committing external data with hashes, and adding a secure gateway to constrain timing, analog, and steganographic channels. It is an architectural proposal, not a fielded treaty system. (arXiv)

#### Architecture C: Off-chip analog monitoring

Possible components:

- rack or server power meters;
- thermal sensors;
- electromagnetic measurements;
- independent clocks and timestamping;
- tamper-evident packaging;
- video or physical inspection of installation.

Potential advantages:

- a physically independent account of activity;
- retrofitting potential;
- evidence that is less dependent on proprietary firmware;
- evasion may require sacrificing performance or changing multiple physical signals.

Load-bearing concerns:

- approximate conversion from physical measurements to compute;
- workload- and architecture-dependent signatures;
- margins of error;
- calibration and sensor placement;
- deliberate mimicry;
- coverage only inside instrumented facilities;
- reliance on inspections to maintain the sensor boundary.

#### Architecture D: Hybrid

Possible combination:

- on-chip identity and secure boot;
- hardware-rooted telemetry;
- independently installed rack-level power measurement;
- cloud or provider records;
- periodic physical inspection;
- intelligence comparison against declared facility capacity.

The learner must test whether this is genuinely layered. A signed meter, signed workload classifier, and licensing system that all rely on the same vendor key constitute less independence than their mechanism count suggests.

#### Cross-taxonomy card: the same signal, a different institution

Use power draw to connect 2.1 and 2.3:

- A treaty-installed rack meter is access-dependent, precise, and maintained through inspections. It belongs here.
- Grid analysis or remote thermal imagery may estimate related activity without permission. That is intelligence-based evidence.
- The physical phenomenon is similar; the evidence pathway, access, confidence, and political role differ.

This reinforces that the course’s four buckets are a pedagogical crosswalk rather than natural kinds.

#### Activity: Bilateral design review

Learners compare an on-chip proposal and an off-chip proposal for the working pause rule. For each architecture they complete:

- what the U.S. side would distrust;
- what the Chinese side would distrust;
- what the operator would regard as commercially sensitive;
- what the verifier cannot afford to leave unknown;
- whose cooperation is indispensable;
- the technical assumption most likely to fail;
- the institutional assumption most likely to fail;
- the most serious abuse or power-concentration risk;
- the smallest pilot that would provide decision-relevant evidence.

The task ends with a constrained recommendation:

Choose an architecture for a voluntary pilot, not for immediate universal deployment. State what the pilot must test before the mechanism deserves a stronger role.

This makes feasibility dynamic and evidence-responsive.

#### Answer-key philosophy

There should be no universal “on-chip wins” or “off-chip wins” reveal.

The model answer should demonstrate:

- the different trust placements;
- circumstances that favor each architecture;
- the likely collapsing weakness under the named adversary;
- what pilot result would move the assessment.
