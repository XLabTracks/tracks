A signature is not a finding, and a tip is not non-compliance. This section is
about the step between them: how a watcher turns an anomaly into an assessment
they can defend, and how much confidence that assessment can carry.

## Observation, Inference, Judgment

Three things get collapsed in ordinary speech and must be kept apart here.

- An **observation** is what the sensor, the filing, or the source actually
  recorded.
- An **inference** is what that observation implies given some assumption.
- A **judgment** is what an analyst concludes given the inference, the
  alternatives, and their own confidence.

Most disputes that look like disagreements about evidence are disagreements one
layer up: about which assumption is doing the work.

## What Breaks the Chain

- **Alternative explanations.** The default question, not the last resort. A
  power draw has more than one cause.
- **Dual-use ambiguity.** The same building, the same chips, and the same
  people serve permitted and prohibited activity. Ambiguity is the normal case,
  not the exception.
- **Base rates.** How many facilities of this kind exist, and how many are
  violations? Around 500 datacenters worldwide exceed 10 MW. A signature that
  fires on all of them has told you nothing.

## What a Source Is Worth

Four questions, applied to every input:

- **Reliability**: has this source been right before, and about this kind of
  thing?
- **Timeliness**: is it describing the present, or a facility as it stood two
  years ago?
- **Corroboration**: does an independent stream see the same thing? Streams
  that share an origin are not independent.
- **Confidence**: what would have to be true for this to be wrong, and how
  likely is that?

## The Progression

The chain runs: **anomaly → verification lead → suspected non-compliance.**
Each arrow is a decision someone has to justify, and each has a different
evidentiary bar. Escalating too fast burns credibility; escalating too slowly
loses the window in which the activity is observable.

What earns a promotion up the ladder is corroboration across evidence
**kinds**: physical, procurement, financial, digital, organizational. They
fail differently, so their agreement means something.

Good analysis portrays uncertainty rather than manufacturing certainty. The
cautionary case is the 2002–03 Iraq WMD estimate:
["dead wrong,"](https://irp.fas.org/offdocs/wmd_report.pdf) with high
confidence, which you will take apart in the autopsy below. The phrase is the
Silberman–Robb WMD Commission's own, and its report is the public
post-mortem.

But first, a question the module refuses to answer for you: in a ~500-site
haystack, how often does an alarm threshold that *feels* cautious fire on
innocents? Commit a guess in the bench before the arithmetic answers.

## The Bounds, in Sum

<SourceQuote
  t="Verification methods for international AI agreements"
  url="https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16074"
  what="p. 9: the honest-bounds sentence"
  by="Wasil, Reed, Miller, and Barnett (2024)"
>

National technical means offer a valuable starting point, capable of detecting
large-scale AI infrastructure and unusual patterns in energy consumption,
hardware imports, and financial transactions. However, national technical
means are limited in their ability to identify software-level violations or
concerted attempts at concealment.

</SourceQuote>

Four independent papers converge: NTM is "a valuable starting point" but
limited against disguise, distribution, and software-level violations (Wasil
et al.); detecting a secret datacenter "is unlikely to be the load-bearing
part of a verification regime" (Scher and Thiergart); imagery, OSINT, and
financial audits are "merely supplemental": though a circumvention attempt
may itself trip other, more reliable mechanisms (*Six Layers*); and military
facilities are a named blind spot (MIRI).

Reframe, not deflation. These bounds are why the layer is supplemental, not
load-bearing, and that is the point, not an apology. Its value is deterrence,
lead generation, and raising the cost and complexity of cheating: each bound
is also a cost imposed on the cheater, and each named gap has a sibling owner
you just placed. The bounds widen over time: efficiency gains shrink the
large-facility signature yearly, distributed development spreads compute
thinner, so the honest modality stays hedged: this layer *reduces* the
effectiveness of cheating; it does not *defeat* it.
