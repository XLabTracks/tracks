Collection from networks and systems themselves — what a facility is actually
running, read from the inside without anyone's permission. It is the one
discipline the AI-verification literature names in a treaty definition and
argues about in the same breath.

## What the Literature Says

*Six Layers* lists cyber intelligence with human and signals intelligence as
the disciplines of its national-intelligence layer, effective without the
Prover's cooperation. Scher and Thiergart rate cyber operations, together with
communication surveillance, High feasibility within a year — the row quoted in
2.3.5, which notes that such approaches are already in use and would focus on
AI developers, datacenters, and chip producers.

The MIRI draft agreement goes one step further and writes the word into its
definition of national technical means: Definition 17 counts "satellite,
aerial, cyber, signals, imagery (including thermal), and other remote-sensing
capabilities employed by Parties for verification consistent with this
Agreement." None of the precedent definitions the draft borrows from — the ABM
Treaty, INF, CTBT, New START — named cyber.

## The Characteristic Limit

That inclusion is the contested part. Read from one capital, "cyber" inside
NTM licenses intrusion as treaty-protected verification; read from the other,
it is a licensed intrusion program aimed at itself, and the word the other side
tries to strike first. The collection asymmetry between the parties is what
makes the same clause read as protection to one and threat to the other.

Everything 2.3.5 said about sources and methods applies here with more force:
evidence collected from inside a rival's networks is the least shareable
evidence there is, because showing what was found shows how it was reached. A
cyber-derived lead can open a file; what resolves it is the regime's own
tools, which is the identify-and-resolve split of this module's opening
applied to its sharpest case.
