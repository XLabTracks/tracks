The stream the field rates highest, and the one with the best real-case
material in the module. Money moves through banks, licences, and customs
filings that leave records, and a covert program spends a great deal of it.

## Procurement, Customs, and Financial Intelligence

The canonical indicator is a GPU-import surge to a region far exceeding
declared facilities' needs (Wasil et al.), with the State Department's Blue
Lantern end-use monitoring and FinCEN suspicious-activity reporting as named
institutional precedents. Wasil et al. list customs-data analysis as an NTM
method of its own: import and export records read for unusual or unexplained
movements of critical hardware, equipment, or raw materials.

Financial intelligence is rated **High** feasibility, under one year:

<SourceQuote
  t="Mechanisms to Verify International Agreements About AI Development"
  url="https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15867"
  what="the locating-compute appendix (pp. 67–80) — the FININT row"
  by="Scher and Thiergart | MIRI (2024)"
>

AI development projects are very expensive, and covert AI projects might leave
a substantial money trail. Financial intelligence gathering might focus on
purchases of AI-relevant raw materials, chips, and other data center
components.

</SourceQuote>

Covert fabs are detectable by monitoring the lithography-equipment supply
chain — a chokepoint of a few dozen capable facilities worldwide (Shavit §6).
The Huawei shadow-fab network surfaced in 2023 through exactly this channel.

Caveats: domestic chip manufacturing erodes customs tracking, and dual-use
purchases blur financial signals.
