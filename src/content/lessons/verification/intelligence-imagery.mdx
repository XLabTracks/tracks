The AI-verification literature does not separate these two disciplines: it
says "satellite imagery" and means both. IMINT is the image; GEOINT is the
image in its geographic and temporal context: terrain, grid connection, change
over time. The collection map keeps them apart because they fail differently;
this section covers them together.

## Overhead Imagery

Building footprint, electrical substations, cooling equipment, security
perimeter. Facilities above roughly 10,000 H100-equivalents typically occupy
substantial footprints with characteristic features (MIRI, Appendix D.3.5; a
2025 snapshot).

Historical note: the IAEA began using satellite imagery only in the 1990s,
after undeclared facilities went undetected under safeguards that covered
declared facilities.

Caveats: imagery is unlikely to distinguish AI from non-AI datacenters
(cooling is the key differentiator), it struggles with industrial look-alikes,
and it can be defeated by underground or camouflaged construction.

## The Grid Connection

Imagery becomes geospatial when the object is not the building but the
infrastructure that must reach it.

<SourceQuote
  t="An International Agreement to Prevent the Premature Creation of Artificial Superintelligence"
  url="https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10783"
  what="Appendix D — methods for chip tracking and consolidation"
  by="Scher, Abecassis, Barnett, and Abeyta | MIRI (2025)"
>

Facilities with this sort of power draw require specialized electricity
infrastructure, such as large transmission lines. This electricity
infrastructure can easily be seen via satellite; even publicly available
satellite imagery reveals transmission lines for facilities like aluminum
smelters and desalination plants which have comparable power requirements. It
is possible to bury transmission lines, but this construction effort would
take a long time and would itself be visible to satellites.

</SourceQuote>

A city-scale power draw needs a city-scale connection, and hiding the
connection is itself a slow construction project visible from orbit.
Converting the draw into compute is the arithmetic of 2.3.3.

## Evidential Value

Imagery can falsify a cover story: Iran's denial of activity at a site was
contradicted by commercially available satellite imagery. Whether commercial
imagery is a net gain for verification or a liability (shutter control,
physical limits, AI-generated imagery) is one of the debates in 2.3.11.

  year="2008"

Read the introduction (pp. 1–3) and "Summary and Lessons" (pp. 28–30); the
sections between them are annotated images and can be skimmed. ISIS located
the bombed building from commercial imagery in October 2007 and identified it
as a reactor; the IAEA's imagery experts, reading the same images, judged that
unlikely. Every signature the skeptics expected (physical protection, a tall
reactor hall, power lines, cooling, a stack, transport, housing) had been
concealed: a remote canyon, a building partly underground, a false roof that
turned a Yongbyon-shaped structure into a box. Imagery did not settle the
question. A related case is Turquzabad,
[Revealed: Emptying of the Iranian "Atomic Warehouse" at Turquz Abad](https://isis-online.org/isis-reports/revealed-emptying-of-the-iranian-atomic-warehouse-at-turquz-abad)
(Albright, Heinonen, Pabian, and Stricker, 2018): a rival's seized archive
pointed at coordinates, commercial imagery showed the site being emptied, and
environmental samples settled it.
