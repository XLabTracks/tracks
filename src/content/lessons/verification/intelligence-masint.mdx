Measurement and signature intelligence reads what a facility cannot avoid
emitting: the heat it dumps and the power it draws. It is the module's most
quantitative stream and the one that decays fastest.

## Thermal Signatures

The signature is waste heat, held in the narrow temperature band of constant
chip operation. It is read by infrared imaging, or inferred from rooftops where
snow does not accumulate in winter (Baker, footnote 127). Sensing capability is
improving: HotSat-class commercial thermal satellites are the current example,
and SatVu has published a
[thermal image of a live US datacenter](https://www.satellitevu.com/news/satvu-releases-first-of-its-kind-thermal-image-revealing-true-operational-activity-inside-major-u-s-data-centre)
in which rooftop cooling, substations, and electrical yards read as active or
dormant from orbit.

## Energy and the Power Grid

Measured energy delivery can be converted into an approximate FLOP count,
which makes large unauthorized runs detectable from power draw; the method
catches coarse violations only (Wasil et al.). The grid connection that such a
draw requires, and what it looks like from orbit, is covered in 2.3.2. This
section covers the number.

Scher and Thiergart rate power-grid analysis at **Medium** feasibility, the
only intelligence mechanism they do not rate High. They give three reasons:

- Covert generation at the roughly 130 MW scale of a 100k-H100 cluster is
  plausible, comparable to military naval reactors.
- Around 500 datacenters worldwide exceed 10 MW (2024–25 figures), so the set
  of candidate sites is large.
- Chip performance per watt improves about 1.6× a year, so the energy
  signature of a fixed quantity of compute shrinks each year.

A case: xAI's behind-the-meter gas turbines in Memphis were detected by aerial
thermal imaging. The case shows the signature and the workaround together.

How the Memphis case was established: aerial photographs commissioned by the
Southern Environmental Law Center in March 2025 showed 35 turbines around the
Colossus site; thermal images about a month later showed at least 33 of them
running; a June flight counted 26. The permit issued in
[July 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/03/xai-gets-permits-for-15-natural-gas-generators-at-memphis-data-center/)
allows 15 generators, 247 MW.

<Callout variant="note" title="Power depends on architecture">

Power consumption depends on architecture, so the same facility can change its
signature without changing its size. Scaling an existing architecture by adding
layers and moving to a new architecture with a different compute profile
produce different curves, and inference and training draw differently again.
See Scher et al., *An International Agreement to Prevent the Premature Creation
of Artificial Superintelligence*, Appendix D, and compare with the account of
development in Module 1.

</Callout>

## Underground Construction

Scher and Thiergart list one more measurement mechanism, rated High
feasibility within a year: geophysical methods already in use to detect
underground construction, applied to a datacenter built underground.

<SourceQuote
  t="Mechanisms to Verify International Agreements About AI Development"
  url="https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15867"
  what="the locating-compute appendix (pp. 67–80) — the underground-construction row"

Various approaches are already in use to detect underground construction
(National MASINT Office, 2022). Applying them could reduce the likelihood of
underground data centers being built secretively.

</SourceQuote>

Their caveat: these approaches may not, on their own, distinguish a datacenter
from any other underground construction.
