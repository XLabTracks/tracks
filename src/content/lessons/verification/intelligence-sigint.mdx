Interception of communications and electronic emissions — who is talking to
whom, and sometimes what they said. In the intelligence-studies canon it is the
oldest technical discipline; in the AI-verification literature it is close to
unwritten, and this section is short because the sources are.

## What the Literature Says

*Six Layers* names signals intelligence among the disciplines of its
national-intelligence layer, in the paragraph quoted at the head of this
module, and says no more about it. The MIRI draft agreement lists "signals"
among the remote-sensing capabilities its Definition 17 counts as national
technical means. Scher and Thiergart fold it into one row with cyber
operations and rate the pair High feasibility within a year:

That is the whole of it. The precedent is richer than the AI literature: in
arms control, national technical means — satellites and SIGINT together — were
sufficient to count large fixed objects, silos and launchers, and treaty law
protected them with noninterference clauses. What that record does and does
not carry over to a training run is one of the debates kept for 2.3.12.

## The Characteristic Limit

Signals intelligence is the most sources-and-methods-sensitive stream, which
is exactly why it is the hardest to share with a treaty verifier: showing what
was heard shows how it was heard. Nuclear treaties impose no obligation on
intelligence agencies to share with treaty verifiers, and sharing is voluntary,
informal, and inconsistent (Baker §2.3.3) — protection of sources and methods
is *why*. Carry that into the memo: a stream that cannot be shown to the other
side can raise a lead, and cannot by itself settle one.
