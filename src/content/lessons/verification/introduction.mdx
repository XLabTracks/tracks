<Video src="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qe9QSCF-d88" title="The Catastrophic Risks of AI — and a Safer Path | Yoshua Bengio | TED" />

Frontier labs and their executives have described advanced AI systems like "artificial general intelligence," or AGI, as highly autonomous systems that can match or outperform humans at most tasks. Beyond AGI sits the stated end goal of several of these labs: artificial superintelligence, or ASI, a system that massively outperforms humans at virtually every measurable task. An ASI would not be one genius but millions of copies running in parallel, each faster than any human researcher, capable of improving its own successors. That last property, recursive self-improvement (RSI), has the potential to make runaway systems. Each generation designs a better next generation, compressing research that would’ve taken decades into months. Throughout this course, we will use the term ASI to refer to AI with dangerous capabilities that pose a material existential threat to humanity. The labs' own definitions and risk assessments vary, and they are worth reading in their own words. Even the people in charge of developing superintelligence, who have the most incentive to obfuscate dangerous capabilities, have expressed public concerns over the catastrophic risks arising from their technology.
