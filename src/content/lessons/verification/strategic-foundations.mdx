
Short reading pathways in game theory and international relations. Learners may test out of any pathway whose concepts they can already apply.

## Strategic actors and credible commitments

<ReadingCard id="sf-jahan-strategic-thinking" href="https://www.imf.org/external/Pubs/FT/fandd/basics/66-strategic-thinking.htm" title="Strategic Thinking" author="Sarwat Jahan and Ahmed Saber Mahmud" year="2015">
  A short, nontechnical primer on players, strategies, payoffs, equilibrium,
  simultaneous and sequential moves, repeated games, and incomplete
  information. A good place to test out of this pathway if these are already
  familiar.
</ReadingCard>

<ReadingCard id="sf-ross-game-theory" href="https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/game-theory/" title="Game Theory" author="Don Ross" year="rev. 2023">
  A deeper introduction to strategic interaction, from utility and equilibrium
  to repeated games and commitment, including the distinction between passive
  environments and strategic agents. Focus on §1 and §6.
</ReadingCard>

<ReadingCard id="sf-fearon-tying-hands" href="https://doi.org/10.1177/0022002797041001004" title="Signaling Foreign Policy Interests: Tying Hands versus Sinking Costs" author="James D. Fearon" year="1997">
  Explains two ways to make foreign-policy signals credible: tying hands
  through future audience costs, and sinking costs in advance, and compares
  their strategic effects. Read pp. 68–75.
</ReadingCard>

## Detection, attribution, and response

<ReadingCard id="sf-ifft-witness" href="https://www.armscontrol.org/act/2005-11/features/witness-prosecution-international-organizations-and-arms-control-verification" title="Witness for the Prosecution: International Organizations and Arms Control Verification" author="Edward Ifft" year="2005">
  Explains how arms-control evidence is gathered, interpreted, converted into
  compliance judgments, and connected to institutional authority and
  proportionate response. Selected sections.
</ReadingCard>

<ReadingCard id="sf-woolf-monitoring" href="https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R41201" title="Monitoring and Verification in Arms Control" author="Amy F. Woolf" year="2011">
  A practical primer on monitoring and verification regimes: treaty
  obligations, technical collection, inspections, confidence building,
  analysis, and political judgment.
</ReadingCard>

## International bargaining and domestic implementation

<ReadingCard id="sf-putnam-two-level" href="https://doi.org/10.1017/S0020818300027697" title="Diplomacy and Domestic Politics: The Logic of Two-Level Games" author="Robert D. Putnam" year="1988">
  Models diplomacy as a two-level game in which governments bargain
  internationally while securing domestic ratification, introducing the
  concept of win-sets. Read pp. 433–441.
</ReadingCard>

<ReadingCard id="sf-hawkins-delegation" href="https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511491368.002" title="Delegation under Anarchy: States, International Organizations, and Principal-Agent Theory" author="Hawkins, Lake, Nielson and Tierney" year="2006">
  Uses principal–agent theory to explain why states delegate authority to
  international organizations, how delegation can enhance credibility, and
  where agency slack emerges. Selected pages.
</ReadingCard>

## Supply-chain structure and bargaining power

<ReadingCard id="sf-farrell-weaponized" href="https://direct.mit.edu/isec/article/44/1/42/12237/Weaponized-Interdependence-How-Global-Economic" title="Weaponized Interdependence: How Global Economic Networks Shape State Coercion" author="Henry Farrell and Abraham L. Newman" year="2019">
  Shows how states exploit control over central nodes in financial and
  information networks through panopticon and chokepoint effects. Read
  pp. 42–61.
</ReadingCard>

<ReadingCard id="sf-wagner-interdependence" href="https://doi.org/10.1017/S0020818300027703" title="Economic Interdependence, Bargaining Power, and Political Influence" author="R. Harrison Wagner" year="1988">
  Uses bargaining theory to show why asymmetric economic dependence does not
  automatically translate into political influence or usable leverage.
</ReadingCard>
