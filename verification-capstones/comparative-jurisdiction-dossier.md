## The brief

Pick one mechanism — incident reporting, pre-deployment testing, model
documentation, compute-threshold triggers, third-party audit — and follow
it through three jurisdictions with real, citable instruments.

The dossier delivers:

- **The matrix.** Rows are the design choices inside the mechanism (who is
  covered, what triggers it, what must be produced, to whom, by when, with
  what consequence). Columns are the jurisdictions. Every cell cites a
  clause.
- **The divergences.** Where the three genuinely disagree, as opposed to
  where they use different words for the same rule.
- **The compliance cost.** What a developer operating in all three
  actually has to build. This is where comparative analysis stops being an
  academic exercise.
- **The prediction.** Which version is likely to become the de facto
  global standard, and the mechanism by which that would happen — market
  size, first-mover drafting, or institutional capacity.

## Why it exists

Institutional literacy is the named gap in governance hiring: technically
strong candidates fail on not knowing which body holds which lever. Tracing
one mechanism through three legal systems teaches that faster than any
survey, because the mechanism holds still while the institutions move
around it.

The repo's `policy-briefs/` folder already holds eight instruments summarised
to a common shape — treat them as your starting index, not your evidence.
Cite the primary text.

## Scope

**In scope:** binding instruments and formally proposed ones. Three
jurisdictions, one mechanism.

**Out of scope:** voluntary commitments and standards-body output, unless
one of your three jurisdictions incorporates them by reference — in which
case that incorporation is itself a finding.

Keep the comparison genuinely comparative: a US/EU pair plus a third that
is neither is worth more than the US/EU/UK default.

## What good looks like

- Every matrix cell has a clause reference. A cell reading "similar" is an
  unfinished cell.
- You distinguish textual divergence from operational divergence. Two
  instruments with different definitions that bind the same set of actors
  have not actually diverged.
- The compliance-cost section is specific enough that a reader could
  estimate headcount.

## Getting started

1. Choose the mechanism by asking which one you could explain to a
   colleague in ninety seconds. Then discover you cannot.
2. Build the matrix rows before reading the third instrument — you want a
   fixed frame, not one that reshapes itself around whatever you read last.
3. Split by jurisdiction in your team, then swap and check each other's
   cells against the primary text. Cross-checking is the exercise.
