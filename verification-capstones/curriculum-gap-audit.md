## The brief

Four open curricula cover overlapping parts of this field, and each one made
different choices about what to leave out. So did we. Find out what ours cost.

You hand in:

- **A coverage matrix.** Rows are concepts, at a consistent grain — roughly
  "a thing a learner could be asked to do" rather than "a topic". Columns are
  the four curricula plus this program. Cells are *taught and assessed*,
  *mentioned*, or *absent*. The grain is the hard part; agree it as a team
  before coding anything.
- **The coding protocol.** How you decided what counts as taught. Two people
  code an overlapping sample independently and report where they disagreed.
  Disagreement rate is a finding, not an embarrassment.
- **The three-gap memo.** The three absences that matter most here, each with:
  who it hurts, what it would take to close, and — the part that makes this
  useful rather than a wish list — **what you would cut to make room.**

## Why it exists

Every curriculum is a claim about what matters, and the claim is invisible
from inside. Reading four of them side by side makes your own program's
choices legible, including the ones nobody made deliberately.

The transferable skill is comparative coding under a stated protocol: build a
rubric, apply it consistently, report where it broke. That is the same move
behind comparing jurisdictions, safety frameworks, or eval suites, and it is
much easier to learn on curricula than on statutes.

Like the tabletop capstone, this one produces a reusable program asset. A
matrix with a stated protocol survives into the next cohort's planning.

## Scope

**In scope:** published syllabi, reading lists and public course pages. The
program's own materials in this repo.

**Out of scope:** enrolling in the courses, evaluating teaching quality, and
ranking the curricula. You are mapping coverage, not judging delivery — the
second needs data you will not have.

**Note on availability.** Some curricula are only shared on request, and one
of the four is maintained by handing it to people who ask. If a syllabus is
not public, say so in the matrix rather than guessing at its contents: an
`unavailable` cell is honest and a fabricated one poisons the whole grid.

## What good looks like

| Dimension | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Grain | "Interpretability" as one row | Rows a learner could be assessed against, consistently sized |
| Protocol | Applied by feel | Written first, double-coded on a sample, disagreements reported |
| The gaps | A list of everything missing | Three, prioritised, each with a named cost to close |
| Trade-off | "Add a week" | A specific thing to cut, with an argument for why it is the cheaper loss |

The recommendation that earns the most credit is usually the one that cuts
something the team liked.

## Getting started

1. Build the row list from *one* curriculum first, then extend it with the
   others. Building it from all four at once produces a matrix whose rows are
   four different sizes.
2. Double-code ten rows before coding two hundred. That sample is where you
   find out your definition of "taught" was never shared.
3. Decide the cut before writing the gap memo. Gaps are easy and cuts are
   where the thinking is.
