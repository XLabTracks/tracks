## The idea, as posed

From [A long list of open problems and concrete projects in evals —
Hobbhahn and contributors (2025)](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1gi32-HZozxVimNg5Mhvk4CvW4zq8J12rGmK_j2zxNEg/edit),
its reader context and the "Safety framework evals" entry. Quoted:

> A number of “ready to go” evals ideas that someone new to the field
> could do within a few days or weeks of effort.
>
> You’re free to just start with these projects and don’t need to ask for
> permission. You can reach out to named individuals but they might not
> have the time to answer.

The "Safety framework evals" entry (credit: Marius) poses the scoping work
itself, as steps:

> Many voluntary commitments and regulatory efforts specify the abstract
> capability they want to measure but do not specify a detailed
> evaluation. Filling in this gap is not trivial but very needed and a
> great way to get good at building frontier evals.
>
> Pick one specific capability (or propensity) from the safety frameworks
> that sounds interesting to you and specify it in more detail.
>
> Write a brief threat model, i.e. which concrete set of scenarios you’re
> worried about and how capabilities relate to harm in that scenario.
>
> Specify what exactly you want to measure.
>
> Design the eval
>
> Run the eval & iterate
>
> Think about how your evaluation would relate to (potential) red lines
> set by the framework.

## What you produce

The entry's own steps, held to a three-week scope: one capability picked
and specified in detail, the threat model, the eval designed and run as a
thirty-item pilot, and a scoping post-mortem that answers whether anyone
should build the full version — the gap-filling the entry calls not
trivial but very needed.
